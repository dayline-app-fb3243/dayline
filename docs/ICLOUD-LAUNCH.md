### Combined Continue flow: production checklist

The prototype now presents one Continue button and Apple, Google, and email choices. The A/B/C email row is a design trial, not a working account resolver. Do not ship the demo Apple/Google sheets as real provider authentication. Replace them with the signed native Apple authorization and Google's official SDK; validate signed identity tokens on the server. The $99 developer membership alone does not create an account backend, Google client, SMS provider or sync.

- Create a secure account service and ownership model that binds a verified Apple/Google subject or confirmed email to one Dayline account. Do not decide that a typed email is "new" or "existing" solely on-device, and do not expose account-existence results in the public UI. Support an established account returning by the same provider, and a safe, verified recovery/linking path for a different provider.
- For email, normalize and check basic format, then send a one-time code or link to that address and verify it on the server before creating an account or revealing any data. A syntactically valid address is not proof of a real mailbox or ownership. A typo pointing to someone else's inbox must not create an account for the typist or reveal the other person's account. Let the person edit and resend, rate-limit attempts, and show a neutral "Check your email" response rather than confirming whether an account exists. Add explicit recovery and address-change verification.
- Verify phone ownership with an actual server-issued expiring SMS code; rate-limit and handle delivery failures, number changes and reused phone numbers. Never accept any six digits in production. The prototype's phone code and email code remain local demo steps.
- Persist a per-account first-setup flag on the server, separate from per-device OS permissions. Only first-time accounts need account setup. Returning accounts on a new iPhone may need the device's own permission prompts and a short contextual explanation; OS-granted permissions cannot be inherited from another phone. Existing installs should check the live authorization state and ask only for missing permissions when a feature needs them, not replay the whole onboarding unconditionally.
- Configure and test real data persistence and restoration by verified account, including local-to-account migration, attachment/media backup, logout isolation, two-device conflicts and reinstall recovery. Current local SwiftData is deleted on uninstall; Demo history is seeded sample content, not restored user data.
- Keep the simulated Google picker only in labeled prototype builds. A production Google sign-in must use the official provider surface/SDK and actual device accounts; do not imitation-clone security UI. A real Apple authorization sheet is provided by AuthenticationServices and must be tested on signed hardware.
- Keep `-demo` fixture data isolated from the empty default build, and confirm that an empty fresh install has no fabricated visits, Day score, or widget value. A seeded Demo is an illustrative few-month history, not proof of sync.

Sources for design/security decisions: Apple's Sign in with Apple HIG (https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple), Google sign-in branding (https://developers.google.com/identity/branding-guidelines), OWASP email verification guidance (https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Email_Validation_and_Verification_Cheat_Sheet.html), Google's email enumeration protection (https://cloud.google.com/identity-platform/docs/admin/email-enumeration-protection).

## Siri and App Intents validation gate

- The prototype declares six **App Shortcuts** backed by actual App Intents: place recall, journal by voice, friend streak, past location, personal streak and day score. Their `perform()` bodies read the local SwiftData store (friend data is demo-only), but the advertised phrases come from `AppShortcutsProvider`. iOS publishes them automatically in Shortcuts; the user need not build a personal shortcut. This is not yet Siri AI's unprompted semantic discovery of app data. The quoted examples in Profile and the hidden six-card demo are in-app UI, not evidence that Siri discovered or invoked an intent.
- David's requested Siri-direct route is a separate launch goal. Inventory appropriate Apple Intelligence schema domains for each real action; add matching schema conformances, `AppEntity`/queries for Dayline visits and journal records where appropriate, Spotlight indexing and donations, and onscreen entity context only when backed by real local data. Test the exact requests on signed supported iPhones. Do not treat a generic `AppIntent` or an App Shortcut phrase as proof of this route. Apple's Journaling domain is explicitly Shortcuts-specific and cannot alone make journal actions discoverable through Siri AI; do not mislabel it as Siri-direct. Keep an App Shortcut fallback only if the user opts for it after the direct path's real coverage is known.
- David's goal is broad, question-driven recall across his visible Dayline history, not six preset commands. The current SwiftData schema has `LocationSample`, `Visit`, `JournalEntry`, `PlanItem`, and `DayScore`. Start with indexed `AppEntity` representations and `EntityQuery` retrieval for visits, plan items, journal entries and day scores, including stable IDs, dates, titles, place/category, status, and user-visible text/score details. Keep the underlying SwiftData store authoritative; index and remove records on every create/update/delete, migration, account switch and sign-out. Test bounded time and name queries, matching relevant records and respecting erased data. Consider grouped entries and current-day computed score as distinct from historical records. Do not index raw GPS trails, home/work precise coordinates, media bytes, private friend data or untranscribed voice as a shortcut to "everything"; first define the requested field-level disclosure scope and device privacy behavior. Media references may require separate entity and permissions handling. Entity indexes are selected projections, not unlimited database read access for Siri.
- Inspect the primary-domain schemas, including system content-open and relevant notes/photos actions, and use a schema only if Dayline truly performs that action. The Maps navigation schema describes a navigation app running the route; Dayline currently hands directions to Apple Maps, so do not claim it. The System search schema currently marks `search` deprecated; check a supported alternative before implementing it. The Journaling schema is explicitly Shortcuts-specific and will not by itself enable Siri AI journal discovery. Scores/streaks have no verified generic primary-domain query schema; no guarantee Apple Intelligence can answer arbitrary questions about them merely because their records are indexed. Test the exact coverage on a signed, supported iPhone before describing it as Siri-direct or removing the App Shortcut fallback.
- Do not delete Shortcuts items just because the simulator shows them. Identify whether they are Dayline's auto-published App Shortcuts or user-created shortcuts first; changing `AppShortcutsProvider` affects Dayline's spoken phrases, while deleting personal shortcuts affects user data. No source fixture seeds a personal shortcut.
- The simulator build checks Swift compilation and the generated intent metadata. Inspect its extracted App Intents metadata and exercise intent bodies from a test harness where practical; a successful build alone does not prove indexing, phrase recognition or actual dialog/snippet behavior in Siri.
- On a signed real iPhone, install the app, check the actions appear in Shortcuts and Siri, and speak every displayed phrase plus natural variants. Test prompts for missing time, friend name, photo count and dictated note; first-run Photos permission; empty timeline and no shared friends; map directions confirmation; and whether the snippet cards appear and remain readable. Retest after reinstall and on a device without Apple Intelligence. Do not claim these pass from the hidden demo or simulator.
- Profile now shows "Use with Siri" on supported installations rather than hiding it based on Apple Intelligence availability. This only shows examples; it does not prove Siri discovery or spoken invocation. Friends are demo-only until the social backend exists, so friend-streak intent cannot provide real friend data yet.
